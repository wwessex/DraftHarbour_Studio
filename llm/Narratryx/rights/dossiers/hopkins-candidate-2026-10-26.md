The Smithsonian's [National Museum of African American History and Culture](https://www.searchablemuseum.com/pauline-elizabeth-hopkins/) identifies Pauline Hopkins (1859–1930) and *Contending Forces* (Boston: Colored Co-operative Publishing Co.,1900). [Ohio University Libraries](https://media.library.ohio.edu/digital/collection/p15808coll4/id/3644) identifies that title-page edition, its publisher, original copyright pages, a full-text HathiTrust link and US no-copyright status. Both evidence pages were acquired and hashed locally. A specific work is under review; this is not a dataset-wide public-domain inference.

[Wikisource's title/copyright transcription](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Contending_Forces) credits Hopkins's prose and R.Emmett Owen's illustrations and cover, and records the1899/1900 notices. Those contributors and materials require separate treatment. Artwork, cover design, captions, page layouts, front matter, chapter epigraphs, poems, scripture quotations, modern editorial material and site apparatus are excluded from proposed literary selection. An old US publication does not by itself settle every contribution's GB status.

[Chapter VI](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Contending_Forces/Chapter_6) offers potentially useful prose around Dora/Sappho's lodging-room decoration, a polite question left unanswered, work talk, a reception invitation and hospitality. These are prospective scene/subtext locators, not extracted approved examples. Proposed selection must keep conversation boundaries and sufficient context without absorbing nearby epigraph/verse/scripture blocks. Historical narration and racial language must be accurately contextualised, not silently modernised or presented as universal factual claims.
