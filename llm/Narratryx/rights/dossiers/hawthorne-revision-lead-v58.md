# Young Goodman Brown revision lead

Nathaniel Hawthorne’s story appeared in the April 1835 *New-England Magazine*, volume 8, pages 249–260. The University of Virginia identifies its later text and page images as coming from the 1846 Wiley and Putnam first edition of *Mosses from an Old Manse*. Google Books identifies the 1835 magazine volume and Penn’s catalogue points to HathiTrust holdings. These are leads for comparing the printed text directly. [1835 volume](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_New_England_Magazine.html?id=vyXZAAAAMAAJ) · [Periodical holdings](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/serial?id=nemag1831) · [1846 edition](https://anthology.lib.virginia.edu/work/Hawthorne/hawthorne-goodman?view=pageImages)

The UFSC item labelled April 1835 is a modern reading copy with a “FREE FOR EDUCATION” notice. Its wrapper and citation do not establish that its wording was transcribed directly from the 1835 pages. Treat it as a locator. The Virginia anthology returned an access error during the text comparison. No source spans or paired targets were added. [UFSC record](https://repositorio.ufsc.br/handle/123456789/132730?show=full)

The next evidence step is to collate the first exchange between Brown and Faith against page images of both editions. A usable pair would need a substantive change in evasion or emotional delivery, preserved scene facts, original wording on both sides, and an exact scoped GB/US record. Publication dates alone do not prove that the exchange changed.

## Opening checked against printed editions on 4 November 2026

I inspected the original magazine opening and following page, and the 1846 book title page and corresponding story pages. The magazine masthead identifies April 1835, and the running page on the next leaf is 250. The book title page identifies Hawthorne, Part I, Wiley and Putnam, New York, 1846. The book story pages are numbered 69–70.

The page comparison covered Faith’s four-turn farewell exchange, Brown looking back, his private remorse and the first exchange in the woods. The original editions use the same lines and scene beats in these passages, with differences in punctuation, capitalisation and spacing. For example, the first edition spells the title before Brown’s surname with a lowercase initial in running text, and the later book capitalises it. The magazine OCR misread a pronoun in Brown’s remorse. The image resolves it to the same word in both editions. These passages can illustrate the existing dialogue and subtext, but they supply no substantive rewrite pair. No source record was approved for training, and the remainder of the story was outside this collation. Images are retained locally under `rights/raw/hawthorne-collation-v61/`.
