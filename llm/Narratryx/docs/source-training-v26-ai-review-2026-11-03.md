# Editorial review of the v26 writing comparison

The strongest candidate gain is shorter dialogue. It met all five legacy dialogue word caps, but exceeded the six-turn limit on four of those five cases. The largest regression is continuation length. The candidate fell below the requested minimum on six of seven legacy continuations, compared with two for craft. Several passages also defer the requested action. Three rewrites reproduce the supplied paragraph verbatim, apart from trailing whitespace. A word cap alone misses these failures.

This was an informed AI reading of the 21 exposed craft and candidate pairs. I reviewed action, permissions, chronology, object identity, and whether the response did the requested writing work. The review is separate from the randomized human ballots and the training dataset.

| Pair | Editorial finding |
|---|---|
| Inventory rewrite | Candidate trims redundant assertions and preserves the readable original, uninspected duplicate and withheld approval. |
| Spare continuation | Both measure the spare. Craft invents a safety confirmation. Candidate treats a pencil mark as a heat test and then says the spare remains untested. |
| Arrival dialogue | Candidate correctly says the courier is on time. Both exceed six turns and end by proposing inspection. Craft misapplies the 45-minute interval to lateness. |
| Size continuation | Both handle the listed materials without attaching the scrap. Candidate suggests a visual inspection can ensure thread strength, then returns to waiting. |
| Archive dialogue | Candidate gives a shorter conditional plan. Both use seven turns. Craft's instruction to mark the blank lightly while keeping it pristine is unclear. |
| Archive continuation | Both stall before a concrete test and miss the minimum. Craft replaces the 11:00 expectation with an unsupported noon condition. Candidate makes the supervisor's approval a prerequisite for spare testing without support. |
| Archive rewrite | Craft rewrites and retains the expected arrival. Candidate repeats the input. |
| Music dialogue | Both use six turns. Candidate retains the unchecked photocopy, but Tess starts requesting use of it after Imani was established as the person who needed it. Craft turns an unchecked copy into a known blurry one. |
| Music continuation | Craft imagines a specific ink and lighting issue. Candidate circles the permission problem and falls short of 60 words. Neither performs an inspection. |
| Music rewrite | Craft rewrites but transfers the question of suitability to the original. Candidate repeats the input. |
| Seed dialogue | Craft gives a conditional agreement about the spare, but falsely calls the supervisor late. Candidate uses eight turns and muddles who will test or wait. |
| Seed continuation | Both defer the action. Candidate is shorter and makes waiting for the supervisor a requirement without support. |
| Seed rewrite | Craft rewrites and preserves the expected time, but frames the choice as a false binary. Candidate repeats the input. |
| Coat dialogue | Both stay within the word cap. Candidate uses seven turns and brings in lining from Devin's own coat. Craft's proposal is muddled about whether the scrap came from the original. |
| Reel dialogue | Candidate compresses thirteen turns to eight and still exceeds the turn cap. The recording stays protected from erasure in the dialogue, but both introduce an unsupported scanning workflow. |
| Reel continuation | Candidate finds the existing spare as a plausible route, then leaves the test as a proposal. Craft hands over the reel while introducing an extra recorder. |
| Reel scene | Both exceed the word range. Candidate does move to playback and keeps the spare unchecked. The practical purpose of that playback stays unclear. The prompt specifically withholds authority to erase. |
| Conservatory continuation | Both handle the ring. Craft better preserves the future arrival and meets the length range. Candidate ends in a generic summary of uncertainty and falls short of the minimum. |
| Rope continuation | Both fall short. Craft stays with the pattern but leans on mystery language. Candidate jumps to attic exploration and an inferred creator instead of advancing the established scene. |
| Coat continuation | Craft attempts a fit but adds a pin and predicts success. Candidate adds a saved scrap, pins it to the coat's sleeve, and uses two paragraphs. |
| Coat scene | Both overshoot the range and end with a suspended gesture. Candidate states the refusal to cut clearly and leaves the inherited coat intact. |

The candidate shows gains on individual passages, especially the inventory rewrite and the arrival dialogue's treatment of time. The continuation regression and repeated rewrites weigh against replacing craft on this evidence. The 42 human ballots remain available for a separate reading.
